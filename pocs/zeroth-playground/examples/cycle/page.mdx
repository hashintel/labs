One step of a plain net fires every enabled transition once, in record order. A firing takes its tokens at once; the tokens it produces land at the end of the step.

<Figure caption="One step from A 1, B 0. Go takes the token from A at once, and it lands in B at the end of the step. Back finds B empty and waits.">
  <OneStep />
</Figure>

The compiler writes that step as the `next` method of one class, with an Int per place:

```python
fire_Go = A >= 1
A = ite(fire_Go, A - 1, A)
fire_Back = B >= 1
B = ite(fire_Back, B - 1, B)
A = ite(fire_Back, A + 1, A)
B = ite(fire_Go, B + 1, B)
```

Four lines test and take in order; the last two land the tokens.

### Options

- **Shape** on modular splits the step into one module per transition and one per place. `Place_A` applies `ite(X(fire_Go), A - 1, A)`, where `X(fire_Go)` reads what `Transition_Go` wrote this step. A variable has one writer in Zeroth, so each place gets its own module.
